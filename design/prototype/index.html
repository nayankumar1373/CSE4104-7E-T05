What are the four necessary conditions for a deadlock?
A deadlock can occur only when all four conditions hold at the same time:
  • Mutual exclusion – a resource is used by one process at a time p. 12
  • Hold and wait – a process holds one resource and waits for another p. 12
  • No preemption – resources cannot be taken away p. 13
  • Circular wait – processes wait for each other in a cycle p. 13
Source · page 12: “A deadlock can arise only if four conditions hold simultaneously in a system…”

3.2 Necessary Conditions for Deadlock

A deadlock can arise only if four conditions hold simultaneously in a system:
1. Mutual exclusion – at least one resource must be held in a non-sharable mode.
2. Hold and wait – a process holding at least one resource is waiting to acquire additional resources held by other processes.
3. No preemption – a resource can be released only voluntarily by the process holding it.
4. Circular wait – a set of waiting processes {P0, P1, …, Pn} exists such that P0 waits for P1, …

1. Why normalize?

  • Reduces repeated data and update problems.
  • Keeps data consistent across tables.

2. Normal forms

  • 1NF – every column holds atomic values; no repeating groups.
  • 2NF – 1NF and no partial dependency on a composite key.
  • 3NF – 2NF and no transitive dependency.
  • BCNF – every determinant is a candidate key.

3. Quick example

  • Student(ID, Name, Dept, DeptHead): DeptHead depends on Dept, so split into two tables.

Explain a semaphore like I am new to operating systems.
Think of a semaphore as a counter for a shared resource.
  1. The idea. Imagine a library with 3 study rooms. A sign shows how many rooms are free. That sign is the semaphore.
  2. wait() / P. A student enters and the number goes down by 1. If it shows 0, the student must wait.
  3. signal() / V. When a student leaves, the number goes up by 1 and a waiting student can enter.
  4. Why it matters. It stops two processes from using the same resource at the same time.
